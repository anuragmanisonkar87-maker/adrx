# NMC Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication (2026)

Source: National Medical Commission, Ethics & Medical Registration Board.
Public Notice No. R-13014/01/2024-Ethics, dated 06/10/2026. In force with immediate effect.
Subject: Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication by Hospitals/Medical
Institutions and Registered Medical Practitioners. Framed in view of Writ Petition (Civil)
No. 1160/2023 (Supreme Court of India) and the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct,
Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002.

Objectives of the notice: preserve dignity of the medical profession; prevent commercialization
of health care; protect patient interest and prevent misleading medical claims; keep healthcare
communication informational, ethical and evidence-based; align hospital advertising with the
standards for Registered Medical Practitioners; promote transparency while discouraging
commercialization and regulating digital healthcare promotion; ensure ethical parity across
all systems of medicine.

## 1. Short Title and Commencement
1.1 These Guidelines may be called the "Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication
by hospitals/medical institutions and Registered Medical Practitioners".
1.2 They come into force on the date of their publication by the National Medical Commission.

## 2. Preamble and Objectives
2.1 The Guidelines regulate advertisements and digital communications relating to medical practice
and healthcare services in an ethical, truthful, transparent and responsible manner. They protect
patients and the public from misleading or unsubstantiated claims, safeguard patient privacy and
autonomy, and uphold the dignity and integrity of the medical profession. They aim to:
a) provide a contemporary framework for ethical, truthful, transparent and responsible regulation of
   advertisements and promotional communications relating to medical practice and healthcare services;
b) safeguard patients, the public and public health by preventing false, misleading, exaggerated,
   deceptive or unsubstantiated medical claims, and ensure that information disseminated by RMPs and
   medical institutions is factual, accurate, transparent and capable of verification;
c) prevent direct or indirect solicitation of patients and commercial promotion that may induce
   unnecessary investigations, treatments or medical procedures;
d) regulate advertisements and promotional communications disseminated through digital and emerging
   technologies;
e) uphold patient autonomy and privacy and maintain the dignity, integrity and ethical standards of the
   medical profession; and
f) provide an operational framework consistent with the NMC Act, 2019, the IMC (Professional Conduct,
   Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, and other applicable laws, without overriding them.
2.2 Nothing in these Guidelines permits any act prohibited under applicable law or the Regulations.

## CHAPTER I - PRELIMINARY

### 3. Definitions
3.1 "Act" means the National Medical Commission Act, 2019.

3.2 "Advertisement" means any form of communication, promotion, publicity, announcement,
representation or dissemination of information in any medium, which directly or indirectly solicits,
promotes or is intended to promote the professional services, reputation, skills, qualifications,
achievements, facilities or commercial healthcare services of an RMP or hospital/medical institution.
Digital, social-media, sponsored, targeted, influencer-led, search-engine, platform-based,
audio-visual, AI-generated or AI-assisted promotional communications are treated as advertising where
their content or manner of dissemination has a promotional character.

- Explanation I: Information given in the normal course to the public at large, without being
  promotional and without any intent for commercial benefit, is exempted from the definition of
  "Advertisement".
- Explanation II: Information regarding availability or installation of equipment, technology,
  diagnostic facilities or services may be disseminated as factual information, provided it does not
  contain claims of superiority, guaranteed accuracy, guaranteed outcomes, comparative claims or other
  misleading or unverifiable representations.
- Explanation III: An RMP publishing any electronic media post should transparently disclose their
  name, qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR registration number.
- Explanation IV: Clinical establishments publishing any electronic media post should transparently
  disclose the names of the RMPs with qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR registration number.
- Explanation V: No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall solicit, purchase, procure, manipulate or
  cause to be published fake, paid or misleading patient reviews, ratings, testimonials, endorsements or
  recommendations for the promotion of medical services. An RMP shall not request or share patient
  testimonials, recommendations, endorsements or reviews for professional promotion on social media.

3.3 "Artificial Intelligence" or "AI" means a self-learning automated process, program, machine or
computer built for doing tasks which usually require the human brain.

3.4 "Hospital/Medical Institution" means a clinical establishment providing diagnosis, treatment or care
of illness, injury, deformity, abnormality or pregnancy (in-patient or out-patient), in any recognised
system of medicine, including institutions under Section 2(c)(i) of the Clinical Establishments
(Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 or any State Act.

3.5 "Medium of advertisement" includes (not limited to) print media, television, radio, social media
platforms, websites and blogs, messaging platforms (WhatsApp, Telegram, Facebook etc.), podcasts,
influencer marketing, digital marketing campaigns, sponsored posts, AI-generated promotion, public
interviews, news articles intended for promotion, online healthcare platforms, and any audio-visual or
digital communication by whatever name called.

3.6 "Social Media" includes all internet-based websites, apps etc. that allow users to create, share and
join online communities and participate in social networking.

3.7 "Registered Medical Practitioner(s)" or "RMP(s)" means medical practitioners registered under the
NMC Act whose names are in the National Register (Section 31 of the NMC Act) or a State Medical Register.

### 4. Scope & Applicability
4.1 The Guidelines apply to the professional conduct of RMPs in relation to advertising, publicity,
digital communication and promotional activities, and address the gap created by electronic media.
4.2 For RMPs, they are read with the IMC (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002
and the NMC Act, 2019; in an irreconcilable conflict the statutory provisions prevail.
4.3 For hospitals/medical institutions, they are read with the applicable Clinical Establishment Act or
Rules or State Act; in an irreconcilable conflict that Act/Rules prevail.
4.4 Publishing through a digital platform, third party, advertising agency, influencer or other
intermediary does not, by itself, absolve an RMP or medical institution of responsibility for content
it authorized, commissioned, sponsored, adopted or knowingly permitted.

### 5. Public Awareness: Public health education
5.1 Medical practitioners may participate in health awareness programmes, public health campaigns,
academic discussions and educational content, provided these do not promote personal practice, do not
solicit patients and are not monetized through promotional marketing.
5.2 Hoardings/billboards in public spaces let out by municipal or other authorities (state transport,
railways, airports etc.) may be used by hospitals/medical institutions and RMPs only for factual
information about infrastructure, facilities etc. Unverified or unverifiable claims are construed as
being for commercial gain and are prohibited.

### 6. Patient Privacy and Consent
6.1 Patient information, photographs, videos, medical records, clinical images, treatment details,
testimonials or other identifiable information shall not be used for advertising except where permitted
by applicable law with the necessary consent and safeguards.
6.2 Patient consent does not, by itself, make permissible any testimonial, endorsement, promotional
patient story, before-and-after depiction, success claim or other advertising practice that is
otherwise prohibited.
6.3 Where consent is legally required it must be specific, informed, voluntary, documented and
verifiable, and comply with privacy and data-protection law.
6.4 Even where disclosure is lawful, reasonable safeguards must prevent unnecessary disclosure of names;
crop, blur or black out faces, birth marks/identification marks or other information that could identify
the patient.

## CHAPTER II - PROHIBITION AND EXEMPTION

### 7. Prohibited Advertising Practices for RMP / Clinical Establishments
7.1 RMPs/clinical establishments should use social media in the most responsible manner, balancing
public dissemination of information with patient integrity and strict professional ethics.
7.2 AI-generated promotional campaigns for furtherance of commercial interest are prohibited. Any
AI-generated promotional content that otherwise conforms to these Guidelines, the Regulations and the
applicable Clinical Establishment law shall compulsorily carry a source mark explicitly stating that
the origin of the content is AI.
a) AI-generated or AI-assisted content shall not be used to create or disseminate misleading, deceptive
   or unverifiable representations concerning diagnosis, treatment, clinical outcomes, professional
   qualifications, patient experiences or healthcare services.
b) AI shall not be used to create or manipulate a patient's image, testimonial, voice or clinical
   outcome, or to create a synthetic endorsement or representation purporting to be that of an actual
   patient, RMP or other person.
c) Where AI-generated or materially AI-altered content is used and its artificial nature may be material
   to the audience's understanding, appropriate disclosure shall be made.
d) Any use of patient information as input for an AI system shall comply with privacy, confidentiality,
   data-protection and professional-ethics requirements.
7.3 Prohibited practices relating to advertising, publicity, solicitation, endorsements, testimonials,
patient images, misleading claims and digital manipulation are governed by Chapter III. RMPs and
clinical establishments must also comply with the patient-privacy, digital-communication and AI
requirements of this Chapter.
Explanation: All campaigns on programmes/schemes of the Government of India and State Governments in
larger public interest are exempted from these Guidelines.
7.4 RMPs/clinical establishments shall not share any patient data, such as names, faces, anomalies etc.,
which can be identified publicly.
7.5 Nothing permits live broadcast or commercial dissemination of surgical or clinical procedures in a
manner inconsistent with NMC guidelines/advisories on live surgery broadcasts. Any live surgery
broadcast must comply with the NMC framework on patient safety, consent, privacy, educational purpose,
sponsorship, commercialization and professional conduct.
7.6 Advertisements involving processing, publication, storage or disclosure of digital personal data
must comply with the IT Act, 2000 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its 2025 Rules.

## CHAPTER III - RESPONSIBILITIES AND ETHICAL COMMUNICATION STANDARDS

### 8.1 Advertising Restrictions and Permitted Communications for RMPs
i. Shall not engage in advertisements/activities intended to create unnecessary demand for medical
   procedures, promote unnecessary diagnostics, or promote fear-based marketing.
ii. Shall not solicit patients directly or indirectly, promote his/her professional services through
   self-promotion, engage any third party for marketing medical services, permit the use of their name,
   image, voice, testimonial or endorsement for promotion of services or any products, or participate
   in promotional campaigns giving an impression of commercialization of medical practice.
iii. Shall not advertise personal achievements, professional success rates or number of patients
   treated, with assertions such as "Guaranteed cures", "Best doctors", "No.1 Specialist",
   "100% success", "painless treatment", "Miracle treatment", "Exclusive cure" or any other unverifiable
   or exaggerated claims by whatever name called.
iv. Shall not give, directly or indirectly, any approval, recommendation, endorsement, certificate,
   report or statement concerning any drug, medicine, remedy, therapeutic article, apparatus, appliance,
   medical device, diagnostic product, health product or commercial product for use in advertising or
   publicity with his/her name, signature, photograph, voice or professional status, whether or not
   any consideration is received.
v. Shall not publish cases for promotional purposes, advertise surgical results, display "before and
   after" photographs, publicize celebrity patients, or promote personal success stories, unless
   published strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymized patient consent.
vi. Shall not publish any statement that creates unrealistic expectations, misrepresents treatment
   outcomes, conceals risks, or promotes unproven therapies, secret remedies or treatments whose
   composition, scientific basis, safety or efficacy is not adequately established or which is
   otherwise prohibited by law.
vii. No RMP shall offer, pay, solicit, receive or participate in any commission, rebate, bonus, gift,
   gratuity, referral fee, lead-generation fee or other consideration in relation to promotion or
   advertisement of medical services, where it is connected with the referral, recommendation or
   procurement of patients, specimens or materials.
viii. Engagement of advertising agencies, digital marketing agencies, influencers or online platforms
   shall not be structured so that payment is linked to the procurement or referral of individual
   patients.
ix. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall make comparative or superiority claims such as "best",
   "No.1", "leading", "most trusted", "top", "number one", "unmatched" or similar, unless the claim is
   objectively verifiable, based on a transparent and independently ascertainable methodology, and is
   otherwise permissible under law.
   Proviso: Any ranking or award relied upon in advertising must be independently verifiable, and its
   material conditions, methodology, relevant date and the awarding organization must be disclosed
   where necessary to avoid misleading the public.
x. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall use discounts, limited-period offers, contests, coupons,
   gifts, cashbacks, referral benefits, free procedures or similar inducements in a manner likely to
   encourage unnecessary consultation, investigation or treatment, or that amounts to solicitation of
   patients. Any lawful disclosure of charges, packages or fees must be factual, transparent and not
   misleading.
xi. No RMP shall use or permit any celebrity, influencer, patient, employee or third party to promote
   professional services through prohibited testimonials, endorsements or recommendations. Any material
   commercial relationship in lawful public-health or informational communication must be transparently
   disclosed. No RMP or institution may use a third party to do indirectly what it cannot do directly.
xii. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall procure or manipulate (or cause a third party to) fake
   followers, likes, reviews, ratings, testimonials, comments, views or other digital engagement, or
   manipulate search rankings, visibility or algorithms to create a misleading impression of
   professional standing.

### 8.2 Exceptions from Prohibitions for RMPs
i. Research-based communication, i.e. case studies published in reputed medical journals, is allowed.
ii. An RMP may make a formal announcement regarding: (a) starting practice; (b) change of type of
   practice; (c) change of address; (d) temporary absence from duty; (e) resumption of another practice;
   (f) succeeding to another practice; (g) public declaration of charges.
iii. RMPs may deliver lectures or talks on public health and diseases for public awareness through
   television, radio and electronic media in their own name and designation, without promoting their
   employer organization.
iv. RMPs may issue health-education and general-information pamphlets/brochures to patients visiting
   their clinic/hospital, but these must not contain guarantees of cure, superiority over others,
   unsubstantiated claims or misleading information.
v. Greetings on days of national importance or related to the medical profession may be given, but must
   not contain advertisement for professional gain.

### 8.3 Hospital/Medical Institution Advertising
i. Hospitals/medical institutions may disseminate factual, objective and verifiable information about
   their name, location, contact details, departments, facilities, equipment, services, emergency
   services, accreditation status, charges and other institutional information permitted by law.
ii. Such communication shall not contain patient solicitation, inducement, comparative superiority
   claims, guarantees of cure or treatment outcome, unverifiable or exaggerated claims, or promotional
   portrayal of an individual RMP. The form, medium or frequency of communication shall not convert
   factual information into solicitation or promotional self-aggrandizement.
iii. Where advertising is permitted by law, it must stay within the scope of that law and these Guidelines.

## CHAPTER IV - MISCELLANEOUS DO'S AND DON'TS

### 9. Responsibilities of Hospital/Medical Institutions and RMPs
9.1 Clinical establishments may advertise only factual information limited to objective and verifiable
particulars: name of the establishment, contact details, departments functioning, diagnostic facilities,
emergency services, accreditation status, other facilities offered and applicable fees. A hospital
website directory of its doctors (names, recognised qualifications, specialties, registration details and
availability) that serves a patient-information function is materially distinct from a paid or
promotional advertisement or inducement.
9.2 Use of the photograph/image of an RMP is not permitted where it constitutes self-advertisement,
solicitation or promotional publicity. Use of self-photographs or similar publicity material on the
letterhead or signboard of a consulting room or clinical establishment is governed by Regulations 6.1.1
and 6.1.2 of the IMC Regulations, 2002 and regarded as unethical conduct.
9.3 An RMP can issue a formal announcement that is factual, non-promotional and limited in scope regarding
starting of practice, change of address, change of practice type, temporary absence, resumption of
practice, and public declaration of consultation fees.
9.4 All healthcare platforms hosting RMPs shall ensure compliance with NMC ethical advertising norms,
prohibit paid ranking of doctors, and maintain transparency in listing practices.

## CHAPTER V - OVERSIGHT AND ENFORCEMENT

### 10. Penalties / Corrective Measures
10.1 Violations are acted upon by the concerned State Medical Council (SMC) under the NMC Act. Graded
action that may be contemplated:
i. First violation: warning and mandatory ethics training.
ii. Second violation: censure and monetary penalty.
iii. Third violation: suspension of registration (3-6 months).
iv. Serious violation (misleading cure claims, inducement for patients, digital mass solicitation, etc.):
   suspension of 6-12 months.
v. Repeated violations: removal from the medical register for 1-3 years.
Hospitals/medical institutions engaging in unethical advertising are dealt with under the applicable
Clinical Establishment Act/Rules/State Act.
10.2 Penalties are imposed only after a show-cause notice and an opportunity to explain.
10.3 The SMC issues a reasoned and speaking order (violation identified, the RMP's contentions and reasons
for accepting/rejecting them, and the basis for the penalty).

### 11. Appellate Mechanism
11.1 An aggrieved RMP may appeal to the Appellate Authority (EMRB, NMC) within 60 days under Section 30(3)
of the NMC Act, 2019.
11.2-11.3 The Appellate Authority examines independently and passes a reasoned and speaking order.
11.4 A second appeal lies under Section 30(4) of the NMC Act within sixty days.
11.5 For violations by a hospital/medical institution, the Clinical Establishments Act penalties and appeals
apply; an RMP who authorized, participated in or endorsed the prohibited communication may separately be
examined by the SMC, EMRB or NMC.

### 12. Power to Issue Clarifications
The NMC may issue clarifications, advisories, SOPs or procedural instructions for uniform implementation.
